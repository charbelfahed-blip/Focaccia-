Your pan. Your flour. Your focaccia.

1. Choose your batch

A starting estimate: 0.55 / 0.70 / 0.85 g flour per cm² of pan. Actual baked height depends on hydration and proofing. Use a pan with enough room for the dough to rise.

2. Adjust your dough

The remainder is all-purpose flour. Whole wheat replaces part of the total; it isn't added on top.

Whole wheat may need more water. Adjust gradually to suit your flour; high whole-wheat amounts give a different, denser crumb.

0.5% (2.5 g per 500 g flour) is an adjustable starting estimate for roughly 24 hours of cold fermentation in a fridge around 4°C, followed by a room-temperature proof. It is not a timing guarantee: temperature, yeast freshness and time outside the fridge matter. Let the dough become puffy and bubbly before baking. This amount is for instant dry yeast, not fresh yeast.

All percentages are based on total flour weight.

Your ingredients

Olive oil shown is for the dough. Oil for the pan and toppings is extra. Use a precision scale for small yeast quantities.

3. Cold-fermentation guide ❄️

Mix today, bake tomorrow. Use the ingredient quantities above. Plan for about 1½ hours before refrigeration, 18–24 hours in the fridge, then roughly 2–4 hours for the final proof.

This starting method suits 75–80% hydration, 0–40% whole wheat, 2% salt and 0.5% instant yeast. Changing those settings changes dough handling and fermentation speed. Follow the dough’s appearance, not just the clock.

  1. Mix · 5 minutes

    Combine both flours, instant yeast and salt. Add cool or room-temperature water and the dough’s olive oil. Stir until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for 20–30 minutes. The dough should be sticky; avoid adding flour simply to make it easy to handle.

  2. Build strength · 3 sets of folds

    With wet hands, lift one edge gently and fold it into the middle. Turn the bowl and repeat on all four sides: that is one set. Cover and repeat twice more, 20–30 minutes apart. Stop stretching before the dough tears. It should become smoother and hold its shape better.

  3. Refrigerate · 18–24 hours

    Place in a lightly oiled container with room to at least double. Cover well so the surface stays moist; mark the starting level. Refrigerate after the folds, around 4°C / 39°F. Do not wait for a full doubling first. The dough may rise slowly and develop bubbles; limited growth in a cold fridge is not necessarily a problem.

  4. Transfer to the pan

    Oil the bottom and sides of a metal baking pan generously—this oil is extra to the calculator amount. Gently ease the cold dough into it, keeping its bubbles. Turn it once to coat. Let it relax for 20–30 minutes, then coax it toward the edges. If it springs back, cover, wait another 15–20 minutes, and try again. Do not force or roll it flat.

  5. Final proof · usually 2–4 hours total in the pan

    Cover loosely and leave around 21–24°C / 70–75°F, including the relaxation time above. Cold rooms or a large batch may take longer. Ready dough looks noticeably expanded and airy, has bubbles at the surface or edges, and jiggles when you gently shake the pan. If it is still tight and dense, give it more time.

  6. Preheat, top and dimple

    Near the end of proofing, preheat a conventional oven to 230°C / 450°F. Drizzle the dough with extra olive oil. With oiled fingertips, press dimples down through the dough gently, without flattening the whole surface. Add rosemary and a little flaky salt. Avoid heavy, wet toppings on your first bake.

  7. Bake · approximately 20–30 minutes

    Use the middle or lower-middle rack. Start checking thin focaccia after 15–18 minutes; thick dough may need longer than 30 minutes. Look for a deep golden top, browned underside and a fully set centre. If the top colours too quickly, cover it loosely with foil and continue baking. For a compact countertop oven, check earlier because the elements sit closer.

  8. Cool for a crisp base

    Once safe to handle, lift the focaccia out of the pan and onto a rack. Wait 15–20 minutes before slicing so steam can escape. Leaving it in the pan traps moisture underneath.

Example schedule · tomorrow’s dinner

Today 4:00 p.m.: mix. 4:30, 5:00 and 5:30 p.m.: fold; refrigerate after the last set. Tomorrow 2:00 p.m.: transfer to the oiled pan. Around 5:00–6:00 p.m.: bake when puffy and jiggly. Allow time to cool. This example uses about 20½ hours in the fridge; proofing can run longer.

What if I want 48 hours in the fridge?

Do not assume the same yeast amount will work for every schedule. Longer fermentation generally needs less yeast, especially with a warm fridge or substantial time at room temperature. Establish the 18–24-hour method first; record your yeast percentage, fridge temperature and results before extending it. This calculator does not automatically adjust yeast for time.

Troubleshooting

Sticky: use wet hands for folds and oiled hands in the pan. Springing back: give it another rest. Not puffy: allow more proofing time and check yeast freshness if it never develops activity. Collapsed and very slack: it may have overproofed; handle gently and bake promptly, expecting less height. Whole wheat: start with 20–40%; allow time for the flour to absorb water before increasing hydration.
